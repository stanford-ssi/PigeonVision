# First CM5 camera bench

This is a bring-up record, not a qualification report. Generated evidence is kept
under the ignored `output/` directory on the Mac and CM5.

## Hardware and platform

- CM5: 4 GiB RAM, 64 GB eMMC; official CM5 IO board.
- Two colour IMX900 modules; A is the `i2c@88000` camera path (CAM0), B is
  `i2c@70000` (CAM1). Both J6 camera-1 jumpers are now fitted. The nRPIBOOT
  provisioning jumper is removed.
- Lenses were selected as CIL212; physical markings and adapter revisions still
  need to be recorded. The operator judged focus sufficient for the initial demo
  and mounted both cameras in the printed rocket on September 25.
- Power is from the Mac USB-C connection. The CM5 has a passive cooler. The
  operator subsequently turned on an external fan; the later temperature drop
  must not be attributed to the passive cooler alone.
- CM5 Ethernet negotiates 1 Gb/s. The Mac currently reaches the LAN through
  Wi-Fi, so this is not a wholly wired transport qualification.
- Raspberry Pi OS Lite 64-bit, 2025-11-24, kernel
  `6.12.47+rpt-rpi-2712`, with the FRAMOS pins in `platform/versions.json`.
  Imaging was verified by byte readback. SSH uses keys for account `pigeon`;
  an empty password is not a login method.

## Measured results and fixes

1. Both cameras produced individual and simultaneous 2064×1552 JPEG images.
   The still and preview modes both need the full RAW10 sensor mode. The default
   smaller preview mode caused a format mismatch during the first attempt.
2. Requesting standard libcamera frame-duration limits initially left the sensor
   near 87 fps. The pinned FRAMOS driver requires its private frame-rate control,
   followed by a same-mode reconfiguration to refresh libcamera's exposure
   limits. Both sensors then reported 30,000,000 micro-fps and 33,333 µs frame
   duration limits.
3. In the first one-minute square-output recording, each camera produced 1,793
   encoded frames and eight explicitly recorded startup frame errors. Steady
   capture intervals were approximately 30 fps. Mean total CPU use was 50.75%,
   maximum logged temperature was 58.4°C, and throttle flags remained zero.
   Both finalized MKV files decoded with the expected frame counts. This is not
   the one-hour qualification, and this earlier run does not establish the
   cooling state of every later run.
4. A later stream developed seconds of delay. Metadata timestamps sampled inside
   the paced transport consumer advanced the mux clock ahead of queued video,
   creating unnecessary padding and further backlog. Stamping queue admission
   at the producer removed that feedback. A repeat receiver-age check fell from
   roughly 3.3 seconds to 0.43–0.64 seconds. These estimates include sequential
   SSH sampling overhead and exclude browser presentation; they are not a
   glass-to-glass latency measurement.
5. The operator reported clipped image sides and a tint difference. The square
   output's measured sensor crop is `[256, 0, 1552, 1552]`: it discards 256 sensor
   columns on each side. A full 2064×1552 diagnostic was started to examine the
   mounted lens coverage. A full sensor view cannot recover light that falls
   outside the physical sensor. Colour mismatch remains unqualified; the
   cameras have independent automatic white balance.
6. The `dual-fullsensor-002` frame metadata confirms both cameras use canonical
   crop `[0, 0, 2064, 1552]`, with no extra vertical crop. The Mac decoded both
   streams at 2064×1552. Saved frames show the image circle crossing the top and
   bottom sensor edges. Full-sensor encoding has logged additional queue drops;
   throughput is not yet qualified. The earlier `dual-fullsensor-001` interval
   also contained a native compilation workload and is unsuitable for an
   uncontaminated encoder comparison.
7. Colour telemetry was built and tested on the CM5 (three native checks), then
   verified in actual frame records. An early pair reported red/blue gains
   approximately `[1.611, 2.418]` for A and `[1.662, 2.326]` for B, at 3935 K and
   3970 K respectively. The cameras also report differing correction matrices.
   These readings establish that processing differs; they do not establish
   which camera is more accurate or isolate illumination from camera response.
   `awb_enabled` is unreported and remains null. No manual colour override has
   been applied.
8. Clean full-sensor thread comparisons did not reach 30 encoded fps per camera.
   With three encoder threads per camera, the measured rates were 29.12 fps for
   A and 29.40 fps for B; with four they were 28.94 and 29.33 fps. Both sensors
   continued near 30 fps. The roughly 89-second measured intervals logged
   capture-queue overflow, zero transport packet drops, mean total CPU use of
   77.17% and 75.67%, and no throttle flags. The external fan was on. Adding
   threads did not remove this bottleneck; full-sensor encoding is unqualified.
9. The actual calibration board is DFVision Q18-400-20: 18×18 squares at 20 mm,
   giving 17×17 internal corners. The first B collection yielded 91 complete
   detections, including repeated stationary frames. Coverage spans different
   image positions and rolls, but apparent linear board size varies only 1.33×.
   Additional distance poses are being collected before the independent A set.
   Detection and fit residuals alone do not establish held-out accuracy or rig
   alignment. Moving the entire rigid rocket with the board fixed is valid.
10. A's completed collection spans 345.57 seconds and contains 9,877 encoded
    frames. Sampling one frame every two seconds produced 173 attempts and 81
    complete-board candidates. These candidate counts are not the video frame
    count. Separately, capture metadata accounts for eight startup errors and
    477 capture-queue overflows during this moving-scene run.
11. Preliminary lens fits retained 27 of 40 selected A views and 43 of 53
    selected B views, with training RMS errors of 0.133 and 0.189 pixels. The
    earlier B model, before incorporating the extra distance session, was tested
    with fixed lens parameters on all 41 complete boards from that new session:
    RMS 0.227 pixels and 95th percentile 0.315 pixels. One difficult view has
    1.13-pixel RMS and remains flagged. Initializing these poses from distorted
    pixels as though they were pinhole coordinates produced a wrong solution;
    inverse Mei bearings and a local virtual perspective view corrected it
    without removing frames. The original failed diagnostic is preserved.
    The newer combined B fit has no independent validation yet, since it now
    uses those distance images for fitting. Both outputs are lens-only; relative
    camera alignment and seam coverage remain unmeasured.
12. An optional CPU-frame copy was tested before encoding, between two direct
    DMA-input controls. Each run lasted 90 seconds, with the first ten excluded
    from comparison. Direct encoded rates were A/B 28.95/29.82 fps, copy rates
    were 24.85/25.62, and repeated direct rates were 28.67/29.59. Copying cost
    about 10.6–10.8 ms per frame: encoder send became faster, but copy plus send
    took about 39–40 ms and delivered fewer frames. All runs had zero transport
    packet drops and no throttle flags. Copy mode was not adopted. These short
    tests do not establish sustained qualification or isolate mapping cache
    attributes as the cause of the bottleneck.

## Evidence and remaining measurements

Local evidence includes `output/provisioning/flash-evidence.json`,
`working-platform-inventory.log`, `latency-before.json`, `latency-after.json`,
`output/camera-bringup-002/`, and `output/sessions/dual-30fps-001/`.
Active focus and full-sensor sessions have distinct native session directories.
A ground transport tap spanning capture restarts contains discontinuous session
timelines and must not be analysed as one uninterrupted capture.

The fan was introduced during interactive testing, so subsequent comparisons
must record that change. Formal preset/resolution comparisons, one-hour
qualification, glass-to-glass latency, neutral-target colour comparison, real
optical calibration, and electrically/optically measured exposure synchronization
remain outstanding. Current cameras are free running.
